Self-Managing or Hiring a Property Manager? A Guide for East London Landlords

Should you manage your East London rental yourself or hire an agent? Compare costs, responsibilities and time commitments before making your decision.

Owning a rental property can provide a valuable source of income, but managing it successfully requires time, organisation and a good understanding of landlord responsibilities.

For landlords in Dagenham, Barking and the wider East London area, one important decision is whether to manage their property independently or appoint a professional property management company.

Both approaches have advantages.

Self-management gives landlords greater direct control, while professional management can reduce the amount of time spent handling everyday responsibilities.

The right choice depends on your circumstances, experience and how involved you want to be.

Let's explore the differences to help you make an informed decision.

1. What Does Self-Managing a Rental Property Involve?

Self-management means taking responsibility for organising the day-to-day running of your rental property without appointing an agent to handle those tasks.

Your responsibilities may include advertising the property, arranging viewings, finding tenants and preparing tenancy documents.

Once tenants move in, the work continues.

You may need to:

  • Monitor rental payments.
  • Respond to tenant enquiries.
  • Arrange repairs and maintenance.
  • Organise necessary safety checks.
  • Keep records and documentation.
  • Address tenancy-related problems.
  • Coordinate inspections.
  • Prepare the property for the next tenancy.

For landlords living close to their rental property, these tasks may be relatively manageable.

However, the amount of work can vary considerably.

A property with reliable tenants and few maintenance issues may require little attention for several weeks. An unexpected plumbing problem or tenancy dispute could quickly change that.

2. The Benefits of Managing Your Own Property

One of the main advantages of self-management is avoiding ongoing property management fees.

You also communicate directly with tenants and have immediate involvement in decisions about repairs, contractors and expenditure.

For landlords who have experience, flexible working hours and a trusted network of local tradespeople, this arrangement may work well.

There can also be satisfaction in taking personal responsibility for your investment.

However, it's important to consider the value of your time alongside the money saved.

Managing a property yourself does not eliminate maintenance costs, compliance expenses or the possibility of rental arrears.

3. What Does a Property Management Company Do?

A property management company provides professional support with the administration and operation of rental properties.

The exact responsibilities depend on the services offered and the agreement between the landlord and managing agent.

Depending on the arrangement, services may include tenant communication, rent collection, maintenance coordination, property inspections and assistance with compliance administration.

Some agents also offer tenant finding, referencing and tenancy preparation.

Before signing a management agreement, landlords should establish precisely which tasks the agent will handle.

It's particularly important to understand how maintenance expenditure is authorised, how emergencies are managed and which legal responsibilities remain with the property owner.

A good management arrangement should make responsibilities clearer, not more confusing.

4. Comparing Self-Management and Professional Management

There is no single approach that works for every landlord.

Consider the following differences:

Factor Self-Management Professional Management
Time commitment Landlord handles everyday tasks Many tasks can be delegated
Tenant communication Directly handled by landlord Can be handled by the agent
Maintenance Landlord arranges contractors Agent may coordinate repairs
Rent collection Landlord monitors payments May be included in the service
Compliance administration Landlord organises documentation Agent may provide support
Management fees No agent management fee Fees depend on the agreement
Decision-making Direct landlord involvement Responsibilities are agreed with the agent

Neither option automatically guarantees better financial results.

The most suitable arrangement depends on the property's needs and the landlord's priorities.

5. Consider the Hidden Cost of Your Time

Landlords often compare professional management fees with the amount of rent they receive.

That's sensible, but it doesn't tell the whole story.

Consider how much time you spend dealing with your property each month.

This might include replying to messages, discussing repairs with contractors, checking payments and organising inspections.

For example, imagine a tenant reports a heating problem on a Friday evening.

A self-managing landlord may need to contact an engineer, arrange access, discuss costs and follow up until the issue is resolved.

A managing agent may be able to coordinate some of these tasks, depending on the service agreement.

The repair itself still needs to be paid for, but delegating the administration can save time.

For landlords with demanding jobs, family responsibilities or multiple properties, that time may have considerable value.

6. When Does Professional Management Make Sense?

There are several situations in which appointing a managing agent may be worth considering.

You Live Away From Your Property

If you live outside London or overseas, arranging repairs and inspections from a distance can be difficult.

Having a local point of contact may make day-to-day management more practical.

You Own Multiple Properties

As your rental portfolio grows, so does the administration involved.

Different tenants, maintenance schedules and property requirements can become difficult to coordinate alongside other commitments.

You Have Limited Free Time

Even one rental property can require attention at inconvenient moments.

Professional support may help landlords who prefer not to handle tenant enquiries and contractor arrangements personally.

You're New to Property Letting

First-time landlords may find the legal, administrative and practical aspects of renting unfamiliar.

An experienced professional can help explain the management process and support the tasks included in the agreed service.

7. Questions to Ask Before Appointing a Property Manager

Choosing a managing agent is an important decision.

Before committing, ask:

  1. What services are included in the management fee?
  2. Are there additional charges for specific tasks?
  3. Who handles tenant maintenance requests?
  4. How are contractors selected?
  5. Will I approve repair costs before work begins?
  6. How frequently will I receive statements or updates?
  7. How are property inspections arranged?
  8. What happens if I want to end the management agreement?

Clear answers will help you understand what you're paying for and what to expect.

It's also worth asking how the company communicates with landlords and how quickly routine enquiries are generally handled.

Final Thoughts

Self-management and professional property management can both be sensible choices.

If you have the time, knowledge and resources to manage your property confidently, handling it yourself may suit you.

If you would rather spend less time coordinating maintenance, dealing with paperwork and managing everyday tenancy matters, professional support may be beneficial.

Ultimately, successful property ownership is about more than reducing expenses.

It's about maintaining the property, meeting your responsibilities and managing your investment in a sustainable way.
